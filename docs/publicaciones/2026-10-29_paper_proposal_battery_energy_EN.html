The battery as seen from flight: LiPo versus Li-ion on a real quadrotor, with end-of-discharge ground truth

Battery model identification from flight logs and remaining-flight-time error, measured against real discharges to cutoff, comparing PX4's stock estimator with an equivalent-circuit model.
Kevin Medrano (TIDOP, University of Salamanca) · collaboration with the energy group · platform RJX F450 / PX4 1.17 / RTK
One-sentence idea. Consumption models and "energy-aware" control are saturated in simulation. What nobody publishes is the field measurement with ground truth: how wrong the remaining flight time shown to the pilot is, per battery chemistry, when the pack is actually discharged to cutoff. This drone has already lived that failure (0 % reported at a real 24.1 V) and has everything needed to measure it.

What we ask of the energy group

Battery bench

OCV-SoC curve, HPPC pulses at 2-3 temperatures and a reference capacity test of each pack every ~10 flights.

Model and estimator

Equivalent circuit (1-2 RC + R0(SoC,T)) identified from flight and from the bench; SoC estimator with a Kalman filter (UKF).

Interpretation

Why each chemistry behaves as it does at 3-15 C, what the voltage cutoff leaves unused, and what to recommend to the autopilot.

1 · Why: the gap in the literature and the real problem

Gap 1 · control

The savings claimed for energy-aware control (NMPC 36 %, PID tuning 70 %, battery-aware planning 7.5 %) are all simulated. There is no real-flight measurement of the energy cost of the controller.

Gap 2 · battery chosen

Nobody identifies the battery model from flight logs at 3-15 C, nor validates remaining flight time against real discharges to cutoff. Cell datasets cover 0.1 to 5 C. A thesis on 162 public PX4 logs admits it: "no ground truth for remaining flight time exists".

Gap 3 · dataset

The only open multirotor dataset with current is the 2021 DJI M100 one (closed autopilot). None exists with an open autopilot, calibrated current, RTK, per-motor RPM and pack temperature.

The problem, measured on this drone

Real measurement (23 Jun 2026, old pack): cell voltage drops from 4.2 to 3.26 V under 55 A peaks and recovers to 3.95 V when throttle is released. That drop is internal resistance, and it fools any estimator that looks only at voltage. (Plot labels in Spanish: V/celda = V per cell, Corriente = current, tiempo = time.)

27 Oct 2026, field flight. PX4 reported 0 % at a real 24.1 V (4.0 V/cell, ~75 %): coulomb counting was integrating a bogus current from the ESC. After switching to voltage-based estimation, the percentage swings with the sag under load.

What we want to answer:

  • How wrong is the remaining time (in seconds) 1, 2 and 3 min before cutoff, per estimator and per chemistry?
  • Does the internal resistance identified in flight match the bench value (HPPC)?
  • How much energy does the 3.5 V/cell cutoff under load leave unused in Li-ion versus LiPo?
  • Is setting BAT1_R_INTERNAL per chemistry in PX4 enough to fix it?

Same voltage, same nominal capacity and same mass (±10 %): the only thing that changes is the chemistry. Two packs of each to separate pack-to-pack variation. Semi-solid state ruled out (1-3 C continuous, a 4.5 Ah pack cannot sustain this drone); LiHV is not a new chemistry; LiFePO4 is not used in multirotors.

Hypotheses (stated before measuring)

H1. Li-ion delivers more Wh per gram, but its higher internal resistance makes the 3.5 V/cell cutoff under load leave more energy unused than in LiPo.
H2. PX4's voltage-based estimator (the stock one) is more wrong on Li-ion than on LiPo; coulomb counting with calibrated current is equally wrong on both; ECM+UKF is the best on both.
H3. R0 identified from flight logs matches the bench (HPPC) within 15 %, and setting it in BAT1_R_INTERNAL measurably reduces PX4's remaining-time error.

3 · How it is measured: instrumentation on the drone

Figure 1. Two independent records of the same flight: the autopilot writes its log to the SD card and the Pi records current, voltage and temperature through its own path. They are aligned afterwards using the voltage, which both sensors see. The ESC current is not used (it is invalid: it reads 13.6 A with the drone sitting still).

What gets installed (product photos)

INA228 I2C breakout, 20-bit. Used with an external shunt (the on-board one is 15 mΩ, ≤10 A).
External shunt on the XT60 positive lead. 100 A → 100 mV; dissipates 0.4 W at 20 A.
DS18B20 encapsulated, 1-Wire. One taped to the pack, one for ambient.
Molicel INR21700-P50B: 5 Ah, 60 A, ~70 g. Six in series = the Li-ion pack.
Phase 2: analog power module (Holybro PM02 or Mauch Hall) into the FC's current pad.

Product images: Adafruit (INA228, DS18B20), 18650batterystore (P50B), PX4 documentation (PM02). Illustrative only; the exact part is chosen at purchase.

What the Pi measures (new)

  • INA228 + external shunt: V, I at 100 Hz and energy integrated on-chip.
  • Pack and ambient temperature at 1 Hz.
  • Profile phase (takeoff, hold, up, down, out, back, land).
  • Optional: per-cell voltage with an ADS1115 on the balance lead.

What the FC measures (already)

  • Pack voltage (calibrated to <0.5 %).
  • eRPM of the 4 motors via bidirectional DShot.
  • IMU, barometer, attitude, flight mode.
  • RTK position and velocity (cm) and fix type.
  • Battery log rate raised from 5 to 100 Hz by a file on the SD card.

What the bench measures (energy)

  • OCV versus SoC per chemistry.
  • HPPC: R0 and RC constants at 2-3 temperatures.
  • Reference capacity every ~10 flights (fade).
  • Per-cell resistance with an ESR meter.

4 · How it is flown: three identical profiles, Offboard with RTK

The drone flies autonomously, commanded from the Pi, with the same setpoints on every flight. RTK (2 mm static, cm in flight) makes "the same setpoint" the same physical place. Each flight ends on battery, not on time: when the filtered cell voltage drops below 3.5 V under load, the drone returns to the mark and lands. That is the ground truth for remaining time.

Figure 2. Profiles relative to the takeoff mark, with a fixed compass heading. Every flight: charge to 4.20 V/cell, 30 min rest, weigh the drone, log wind and temperature, fly to cutoff.

Sequence of one flight

  1. Pack at ambient temperature, charged to 4.20 V/cell and rested 30 min.
  2. Drone on the mark; RTK fixed required to take off (node gate).
  3. Pilot sets the switch to Offboard and arms; the node takes off and runs the profile.
  4. If the fix drops to float in flight, the flight continues and the segment is tagged; no abort.
  5. Filtered cell voltage (2 s) < 3.5 V under load → return to the mark and land.
  6. 30 min rest and final OCV reading; record in the session manifest.

Venues and risks

Venues: Journal of Energy Storage or Energies (battery angle); Drones or Aerospace Science and Technology (UAV angle); IEEE Trans. Transportation Electrification. The dataset, separately, in Scientific Data or Data in Brief.

Risks: (1) "LiPo ECMs are already studied": the defence is the 3-15 C regime, identification from flight, the ground truth and two chemistries on the same drone. (2) Small fade over 30-40 flights: reported, SoH not promised. (3) Wind: anemometer per flight, discard gusts, ABBA order.

Second paper with the same instrumentation: energy per mission of the controller (PID, jerk-limited and MPC, already implemented on the Pi) with the accuracy-energy Pareto front measured in the field; the published savings (7-70 %) are all simulated.
